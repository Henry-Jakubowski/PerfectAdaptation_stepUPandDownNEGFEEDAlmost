A 3-Node Negative Feedback Loop

Signal S (clamped/externally controlled) drives production of species A, which is converted reversibly to species B through a saturable enzyme step. B feeds back to inhibit the production of A from S, closing a single negative-feedback loop: S → A → B ⊣ A. Unlike the antithetic-feedback (X-mediated) design, a direct feedback loop like this one generally gives only approximate homeostasis — watch whether B truly returns to its starting value after each step in S, or merely comes close.

About this model Sniffers, buzzers, toggles & blinkers

This ModelBrick is a variant built from the same article as the antithetic (X-mediated) perfect-adaptation model: a three-node network in which signal S drives species A, A interconverts with B through a saturable enzymatic step, and B feeds back to suppress A's production — a direct, single negative-feedback loop rather than the article's separate integral-feedback pathway. S is stepped up in four increments of 0.2 and then back down in four decrements of 0.2, letting you compare A and B's response on the way up against the way down. Originally built and simulated in VCell and exported as SBML.
